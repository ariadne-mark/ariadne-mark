# 0.7.3a4 model-assisted integration pilot

**14–15 November 2026.** This was an AI-agent customer-journey exercise on an
owned Linux server, not an external customer deployment or adoption claim.
The participant followed the public first-export walkthrough and built a small
resident exporter plus a separate verifier. SDK patch count: **zero**.

Runtime: SDK **0.7.3a4**, source
`49578688710a8bae2373aaf9d47ff9521f566d4c`; m6a.large, 2 vCPU/4 GiB container
limit. Development credentials throughout. The exact input corpus and timings
are separate from the [matched CJ-05 resource series](0.7.3a4-performance-2026-11-15.md).

## What the exercise established

- Eleven exports: PNG/JPEG, six-second 720p landscape and approximately
  six-second 1080p portrait video, all three profiles, plus three warm repeats.
  Inputs were deterministic Pillow textures and FFmpeg test patterns, not real
  generative-model outputs.
- A separate verifier process, without the provider signing key, recovered the
  expected IDs from all eleven exports. Development signatures were valid;
  their issuer remained untrusted.
- A pixel-preserving PNG re-save and stream-copy MP4 remux removed metadata.
  The watermark ID and operator registry recovered the original manifest.
  The changed file was not declared cryptographically authenticated.
- Unmarked PNG and 720p video returned `not_found` in explicitly selected
  `current` scope. These two observations are not a population FPR estimate
  or a scan of every legacy scheme.
- Overwrite and unsupported-extension checks preserved existing output and
  produced no success receipt for rejected work. Power-loss recovery was not tested.
- A bounded, single-worker application queued a second export. A small pair of
  nearly concurrent HTTP uploads produced 200 and 429 under the configured
  admission limit. These are control checks, not a concurrent-load benchmark.
- Signed HTTP reports passed public-key/asset verification; wrong asset and
  damaged JWS were rejected. The registry still supported recovery after verifier
  restart. HTTP was loopback; public TLS was not tested in this pilot.

## Timings and their resource boundary

The [original pilot metrics CSV](cj05-client-pilot/METRICS.csv) is preserved
unchanged. Three warm six-second 720p Economy5s exports took 5.426, 5.483 and
5.508 seconds. The first PNG took 6.134 seconds including lazy initialization;
the next JPEG took 0.490 seconds, but it was a different file/format.

**The pilot's CPU is Python SELF only**: it excludes live Node/FFmpeg child CPU.
Its `ru_maxrss` is a process-lifetime high-water mark, not per-job or full-container
RAM. Docker snapshots were not measured peaks. The configured 4 GiB limit was
not measured consumption. Dependency layers were cached; setup/download/build
and human reading time were not reliably timed separately.

Use the [CJ-05 matched series](0.7.3a4-performance-2026-11-15.md) for integrated
CPU, kernel memory peaks, cold/warm comparisons and negative-scan cost. It uses
different fixtures; do not derive a speedup by comparing these two data sets.

## Interpretation corrected after review

The participant initially classified differing `c2pa.record` and
`recovery.record` disclosures as a privacy bug. That classification was withdrawn
after the source policy was checked. The C2PA issuer was untrusted, while the
operator registry tuple was explicitly approved. `redacted_fields` named paths
within the C2PA record rather than promising removal from every source.

Additional policy checks confirmed that empty/unapproved source configurations
hide both records; approved sources disclose the supported registry record.
This is a documented distinction between evidence sources, not a demonstrated
unapproved-source bypass or a general privacy/security proof. See the
[public provenance field contract](../operations/verifier-quickstart.md#public-provenance-fields).

## Still outside the observation

Real content and delivery transforms; visual quality/flicker; production
CA/TSA enrollment and public trust; storage/backup/orphan reconciliation;
long-duration concurrency and failure recovery; legal applicability and
product-specific disclosure. Successful development exports do not establish
production certification or compliance on behalf of an application.
